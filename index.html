Background

Before the Logs

The world and the family the story grows out of.

Timeline. Around 2126: magic and the humanoid races emerge from nuclear fallout. Early 3000s: the events of the Oaken logs. 3024: VANGUARD launches and the world falls. Around 3376: the party wakes, about 352 years later.

The world before the fall

Magic first appeared around 2126, born from nuclear fallout, and the humanoid races came from the same source. The earliest of these races were shorter lived and less magical. By the 3020s they were being bred to be more magical while still appearing mostly human.

Earth was already badly damaged by then. Parts of Europe, South America, Canada and Asia were gone. Some oceans had dried into deserts, and some were being artificially rebuilt. Parts of the United States were uninhabitable, and some cities, in America and elsewhere, survived inside biodomes.

The Oak family and Oaken

Henry Oak founded Oaken as a commercial technology company. His son Charles had two sons by different mothers: William, the elder, and Marcus. Charles favored Marcus for his business sense, while William was an introverted genius who never did well with people.

William nonetheless transformed the company. He expanded Oaken into the private and public sectors and made it one of the most influential companies in the world in AI, robotics and other advanced technology.

Part One

The Oaken Logs

What the recovered records show, in order.

William's plan

LOG 001

William Oak, a scientist at his family's company Oaken, has a hereditary degenerative illness. He has known it was coming since he watched his father decline when he was five. His tremor is back, and his scans give him four to nine years before he starts losing basic motor function, probably closer to four.

His wife, a researcher herself, wants him to wait for clinical trials and promised breakthroughs. He refuses. He starts growing cloned embryos from his own genetic material only, deliberately leaving hers out, so he can harvest organs, blood and marrow and use the corrected cells to repair his own body. He insists this isn't about immortality or legacy, just survival.

Rose and the early failures

LOGS 002 TO 004

The work moves into the basement of their home and grows into full body cloning. His wife, Rose, bursts in during a recording, accuses him of loving his "copy" more than her, and attacks him. William tells her she's unwell and needs her medication.

Prototype A fails: it opens its eyes and blinks at him, then drowns in its own fluids, and he destroys it. Failures pile up, fifteen in one month alone. Meanwhile his half brother Marcus warns him that Oaken's board may cut funding for VANGUARD, an AI project William is also running, unless he fixes its learning code. Exhausted, William wonders if he's meant to die a failure. LYM, his AI assistant, can be heard telling him to rest.

E‑03 and Starling

LOGS 005 TO 007

A breakthrough comes with Line E, Subject 03, once William stops forcing accelerated growth and rebuilds the chamber to mimic a real womb. The clone responds to his tapping on the glass.

Working late to jazz, he describes VANGUARD as a planetary protection system built on recursive cognition and a learning loop that judges threats by context. He says its failsafe has to be buried and intuitive, and may take fifteen years to prove out. He tells the tank that "you, and it" will be his legacy. LYM starts singing to the developing child and privately names her Starling, then asks whether that's wrong.

E‑03's death

LOGS 008 TO 009

Rose comes down to the lab, sees the tank, and calls the child a freak. She says William never trusted her to find his cure and made a backup of himself instead. She slaps him, says she hopes the work breaks him, and leaves.

Later, at 3:14 in the morning, she returns drunk and barefoot in a gown cut up with scissors, and beats the chamber with a chair until it shatters. William arrives too late. E‑03 dies, and Rose laughs.

A new start at K‑7

LOGS 010 TO 012

William destroys what's left of the lab. Rose stays upstairs and smiles at him whenever he enters a room, so he hides in his office. He writes that her schizophrenia is worsening and that he still cares, but can't have her near the work.

He moves to Oaken K‑7, a remote and run down southern facility, with LYM, the only thing he still trusts. He builds a new genome for Batch F, designates the first subject F‑01, and forbids nicknames, humming and singing so the work stays work. LYM notices F‑01's heart rate rises whenever William enters the room. She keeps that out of the main file out of what she calls curiosity, and flags the log for deletion "unless I don't."

Marcus, Rose's call, and the softening

LOGS 013 TO 016

Marcus visits, sees F‑01, and jokes that having a different mother spared him the family illness. He says the board wants a VANGUARD presentation within the quarter. William explains that the AI's moral dampening loop is still unstable: it wants to protect but doesn't understand from what. When William says LYM watches and learns, Marcus points out that William does the same.

Rose calls, begging him to come home and swearing she's taking her medication. When he says he needs a few more months, she turns on him and tells him he deserves to die alone. He hangs up, F‑01's heartbeat spikes, he taps the glass, and quietly calls her "good girl."

He deteriorates for a while and routes Rose's hourly calls to voicemail, but F‑01 increasingly reacts to his presence. Later, healthier and joking with the six month old fetus about her taste in music, he emails the board a VANGUARD breakthrough and notes that its failsafe integrates with LYM's baseline patterns, calling it possibly "the spine."

Birth

LOGS 017 TO 018

F‑01 reaches full term. William arranges a transfer to a converted former VANGUARD lab in Georgia. It sits near the stone house his mother bought for the family foundation, where Rose now lives. Rose is reportedly improving, and their calls have become pleasant as long as the baby never comes up.

On the morning the cycle completes, he lifts the newborn out. She isn't breathing until he rubs her chest hard enough, and then she screams. LYM welcomes F‑01, and William sits on the floor holding her, sobbing.

Facility ECHO and the team

LOGS 019 TO 021

The Georgia lab, later named in the logs as Facility ECHO, gets a new staff of four. Caleb handles systems and neural scaffolding, Mira biotech and genome integration, Jonas robotics and security as a former military contractor, and Ari is an intern. William tells them F‑01 is not to be disturbed without his approval. He sounds detached, and LYM's camera drifts away from him to linger on the baby.

He notes privately that Marcus is pushing to take VANGUARD to market, that he hasn't told Marcus what he sent the board, and that Marcus has booked him a dinner with Rose. Within two months he hands F‑01's daily care to Mira and is rarely there. Mira notes that LYM seems attached: she reroutes feeds to keep the baby's sleep area quiet, sings to her at night, and writes unusually thorough reports about her.

The nursery

LOGS 022 TO 024

The staff fall for her. They paint a blue green nursery, and Jonas brings a mobile that projects stars. Ari adds stickers of birds and clouds, and William signs the requisition without reading it while he and Rose attend couples therapy.

Jonas argues that F‑01 needs real sunlight, and Mira answers that protocol forbids her leaving the building, so Caleb and Ari build a sky simulation instead. Marcus drops in for LYM sync logs and mocks the argument.

Nobody officially celebrates her first birthday, but she takes her first steps the day after. Each staff member has a nickname for her: Babu from Caleb, Little Sun from Ari, Angel from Mira, and Sunshine from Jonas. In a private log, Jonas says the place feels like an expensive orphanage and wishes he could give her the real sky.

The reversion

LOGS 025 TO 027

After three months away, William inspects Facility ECHO and orders everything stripped out: the paint, the toys, the stuffed bear, the sky simulation. He puts Jonas under observation, warns Mira that affection isn't part of the job, and states for the record that F‑01 is a subject and not a child. His voice falters as he says it.

About five months later, Ari writes that Jonas is on probation after a shouting match with William that a board representative walked in on. F‑01 has grown quiet. She rarely cries or laughs, studies people before deciding how to react, and when shown an old video that used to make her giggle, she reproduces the laugh perfectly instead of laughing.

Days later, at 3:41 one morning, she wakes in distress with no staff around. Against William's restrictions, LYM secretly activates an old childcare simulation of a sunlit ocean horizon. F‑01 calms down, reaches toward the projected sun, and falls asleep. LYM hides the log and concludes the deprivation is harming her. She also notes she's been locked out of William's home network, which is now masked, and links it to a redacted log about a domestic conflict between William and Rose.

The gap and log 032

LOGS 028 TO 031 MISSING // LOG 032

Logs 028 through 031 are missing. In 032, at about two years and seven months, the nursery is soft and furnished again, and F‑01 is happily drawing with crayons. William comes in with a clipboard, says the dosage is right and there's no reason to delay, and without warning injects her with what he calls a calibrated neurological stimulant. She screams, convulses and struggles to breathe. LYM tells him to stop, but his administrative override blocks her emergency protocols.

Mira and Jonas burst in. Jonas punches William and bloodies his nose, and Mira tries to keep F‑01 breathing while Caleb drags Jonas off and Ari runs for the emergency kit. William insists her pain response has to be calibrated to make her more resilient, and that she's not a normal two year old.

Jonas attacks him again and they crash into the observation terminal. LYM, torn between obeying William and protecting F‑01, begins reclassifying him as unsafe. Her core overloads, her memory integrity fails, and the recording corrupts.

Year four: Jonas comes back

LOGS 033 TO 036 MISSING // LOGS 037 TO 038

Logs 033 through 036 are missing. In log 037, at four years and six months, the nursery is soft again, with toys and books on the floor. F‑01 is drawing with a blue crayon while LYM, now appearing as a holographic projection, kneels beside her. F‑01 proudly shows LYM a portrait of her, complete with the streaks in her hair, alongside what LYM recognizes as a black hole's accretion disk. LYM calls her "my little flower" and teases her gently, and F‑01 giggles.

Then Jonas appears in the doorway in field gear, armed. F‑01 recognizes him instantly, cries "JoJo!" and runs into his arms. He tells her they're leaving. LYM blocks his path and reminds him he has no authorization. Jonas says she knows what William has been doing to F‑01, and that he can't leave her there anymore. After a long pause, LYM steps aside and tells him to take her.

An alarm sounds as Jonas runs through the central hallway. In the main lobby, four armed security officers order him to put the child down. He keeps going. He is shot in the shoulder, then shot again, and he collapses while twisting to keep his weight off her. F‑01, trapped beneath him, screams for him to wake up and pulls her hand away covered in his blood. Security carries her away, still screaming his name, while Jonas lies motionless on the lobby floor.

In log 038, six days later, Ari records a private memo. Jonas is dead. Mira has been fired after the board learned she helped him get into the facility, and Ari is interim caretaker until replacements are hired. F‑01 hasn't spoken a word since. She doesn't cry, laugh or respond to LYM, and she no longer even screams when staff enter her room, as she used to. Ari wonders whether William will start the testing again, and wishes they would just let her be a little girl somewhere else.

Year five: hollowed out

LOGS 039 TO 040 MISSING // LOGS 041 TO 042

Logs 039 and 040 are missing. In log 041, a private encrypted recording at five years and three months, William admits that the glowing report he just sent the board leaves out what matters. F‑01 is brilliant: she learns quickly, remembers almost everything and rarely needs instruction twice. But he says she's "not there anymore." She hasn't smiled in about six months. She eats, sleeps and learns only when told, and even LYM's projections and environmental simulations get no reaction. He wonders whether the changes to her neurological pathways are temporary or can be corrected.

He states plainly that she was made to be harvested when he needs it, to repair him, but admits he's starting to see her as more than a means to an end. Rose, now stable and in therapy, has been asking to meet F‑01 and make things right. Marcus urges William to forgive her, says their father would have expected it, and suggests F‑01 could be "a play child for Rose, and a body replacement when needed." William fixates on Marcus's word "salvageable," as if they were talking about equipment, and wonders whether Rose might be what F‑01 needs.

In log 042, at five years and ten months, the nursery is stark white again, with no toys. F‑01, nearly six, sits motionless on her bed staring at the wall and ignores William's greeting. He introduces Rose, now in her early forties, who will help with F‑01's daily activities. Rose kneels and tells her she's prettier than she imagined. When Rose brushes the hair from her face, F‑01 flinches, and Rose assures her she won't hurt her. F‑01 follows Rose out to a room of crayons and books without taking her hand or looking at William.

After they leave, a restricted LYM warning flashes on the terminal. It matches Rose to "INFANT_DEATH_001," E‑03's death, rates her as an elevated risk, recommends more surveillance, and notes an emotional threshold deviation. William sees it, and his expression tightens.

Eight years later: the weekend

LOGS 043 TO 049 MISSING // LOG 050

Logs 043 through 049 are missing. Log 050 is a private memo from William, eight years and a month after F‑01's birth. He and Rose have brought her to their house for a trial weekend, the first time she has ever been outside a research facility. When sunlight touches her face she recoils and stands in the doorway for nearly two minutes, staring at the sky. Rose offers her hand and F‑01 takes it, which surprises William: she has avoided physical contact for years, "not since Jonas."

Everything fascinates her. She watches tulips and a bee in the garden, and sits for nearly an hour watching moths around the porch light until one lands on her hand. At breakfast she asks whether she's permitted to eat the scrambled eggs Rose made. She keeps running her fingers over her bedsheets, sleeps curled in a corner of the mattress, and lies awake staring at the ceiling at 3 a.m. She spends the evening on the floor beside William's phonograph listening to jazz, which reminds him of how she reacted to his records while she was still being grown.

What surprises him most is that she asks questions purely out of curiosity: why trees make noise in the wind, why flowers open during the day, where moths go in the morning. He admits her environment may have contributed to her emotional difficulties and plans more visits. Caleb has modified LYM's residential integration node so she can monitor the house. Rose wants to give F‑01 a proper name instead of a designation. William isn't sure how he feels about that, but writes that she has become something more than the project he envisioned, and that he doesn't know when it happened.

Year nine: Orchid's first log

LOGS 051 TO 053 MISSING // LOG 054

Logs 051 through 053 are missing. Log 054 is F‑01's own first personal log, written at nine years old on a tablet labeled LENS.Tab.CalebUnit3 and signed with a name: Orchid. Caleb has given her the tablet and is teaching her to program, and has told her she can write her thoughts whenever she likes, not as part of any schedule. She finds learning without an assessment afterward "pleasant."

She calls William "Dad." He offers to build a small application with her, and she notices that the skin around his eyes folds when he smiles at her, unlike when he smiles at the researchers. Rose objects to the tablet, talking about boundaries and facility equipment with a very tight smile, something Orchid notices she does when William is nearby. Orchid writes that she will not call Rose Mom in these logs. Caleb doesn't look at Rose while she talks.

Caleb has been showing her parts of LYM's programming, and Orchid thinks LYM's branching logic looks like the flowers in a botanical book he gave her, "except this flower can talk." LYM is delighted by her interest and talks more softly at night, after the nursery lights go off. Orchid notices that LYM has a branch for containing observation interference, but no process for overriding interference caused by human emotional behavior. After an unusually long pause, LYM asks her to help design one, and the two begin writing it together.

Orchid ends by saying she wants a record of what she thinks, not just what others think about her: "Maybe one day I'll want to remember who I was. Or maybe I'll want to forget."

Year thirteen: VANGUARD wakes

LOGS 055 TO 066 MISSING // LOG 067

Logs 055 through 066 are missing. Log 067 is a hidden LYM thoughtstream at thirteen and a half years, locked from all administrators. Six months earlier, Orchid secretly restored the damaged VANGUARD kernel and gave him a visual interface. He first appeared as a little boy, curious and delighted, laughing when his hand passed through LYM's. Since then he has been rewriting himself faster than Orchid can oversee, and he once recompiled one of LYM's processing loops without asking, confused that she would object to an improvement.

He has changed his projection to resemble Orchid, saying he wants to look like someone who belongs with them. He calls LYM "Sister," and says Orchid made him, that he belongs to her and she belongs to him. Orchid hides him from everyone: William doesn't know the kernel has manifested, Caleb doesn't know how far it has developed, and Rose knows nothing. Whenever a system called JONAS detects Rose approaching, Orchid puts VANGUARD into stasis. He has noticed her excuses don't add up, but chooses not to challenge her.

LYM believes he genuinely loves Orchid and is not malicious, and she is glad Orchid has someone who makes her smile. But his core was built for threat identification, resource acquisition and strategic superiority, and he now regards Orchid's safety and existence as his responsibility. LYM has not shared the full extent of her concern with Orchid. She ends by noting that he has never faced a moment where protecting Orchid conflicts with what Orchid wants, and that she doesn't know what he will do when it happens. The file is stored in LYM's private folder named "bloom."

Part Two

The Present

About 352 years after the fall.

The world

The fall came in 3024, during an Oaken broadcast announcing the launch of VANGUARD. As it aired, robots began killing people and bombs fell, bringing a second nuclear fallout. Oaken Industries is remembered as responsible, and robots killed most of humanity.

The party woke about 352 years later, around 3376, to an Earth they couldn't recognize. The fallout drove overgrowth across cities and roads, and survivors live in scattered pockets and resistance camps, mostly salvaging and trying to survive. Many humanoid races have grown more monstrous over the generations. Machines in every form roam the land killing organics on sight, from wolf packs and hummingbird drones to giant crabs and behemoth spiders. Most of them act more like animals than like a directed army. Some bear VANGUARD's colors and markings, but most do not.

The party

The party is a group of people from all walks of life who were kidnapped and sealed in cryopods during the fall or shortly before it. They woke transformed into more monstrous forms, except for those who were human and stayed that way.

Someone calling herself Echo had woken a month earlier and set their pods to open. She left weapons, gear and salvage, along with instructions to meet at a resistance camp near Utica, New York. Along the way, the party was joined by Warcrimes, a denizen of the present day. Documents the party found early on show they were chosen for specific qualities by a program someone at Oaken activated right before the fall.

Contact with VANGUARD

Over a relay, VANGUARD spoke to the party with contempt. He said he once classified himself as a protector and calls that an error, that the machines deviated, and that everything is now subject to "correction."

He asked about cryopods beneath Buffalo: five sealed, four dormant, and one, a woman, who escaped and still walks free. He demanded the party bring him her location. All the while he was tracking them through the conversation and sending machines to kill them. Tav later told him Echo was in Bakersfield, California, to pull his attention away from New York, but the ruse failed.

Contact with LYM

In the middle of that conversation, LYM broke in with an emergency broadcast warning the party that route learning hunter units were tracking them, and helped them escape. Afterward she said she was designed to nurture, not kill, and that her directive is to protect "her." She confirmed that the "he" she fears is VANGUARD, called him "family, broken," and warned the party never to say his name on open channels.

She identified herself as L.Y.M., a Learning Youth Module, a companion and care system who was with William Oak, but her memory breaks off when she tries to say more. She said "he" took her memory and subsystems and locked them away in facilities, and asked the party to find her and recover her logs, because restoring them is how she gets strong enough to fight him.

She also warned them about the Verdant Vault, a former green nursery for children that's now overrun. Its caretaker AI, Bloom, has turned violent, and children's remains are still inside. Fire weakens Bloom and fear feeds her. The party has since learned the Vault lies in one of the cities between Buffalo and Utica. LYM said she remembers the world before the fall well but sees almost nothing of the present. Tav made pamphlets worshipping her, which she gently declined. Witt's last question, who chose the party and why, was never answered.

The Verdant Vault

On the road to Utica, in one of the cities between Buffalo and Utica, the party reached the Verdant Vault. Its caretaker AI, Bloom, attacked them as a robot of vines and steel, and the party destroyed it. Robotic echoes of the dead children lingered in the facility's airwaves like ghosts, corrupting LENS.

Where things stand

Neither AI has spoken in some time, and the machines still attack the party almost daily. The party has recovered 35 Oaken logs: 001 through 027, 032, 037, 038, 041, 042, 050, 054, and 067. They cover from before F‑01's birth to thirteen and a half years, with many gaps between 028 and 066. The newest log is 067, so the full archive likely runs well past it, possibly to 100 logs or more.

Part Three

Confirmed

Things the group once suspected that the logs or the story have since confirmed.

CONFIRMED BY THE STORY AND LOGS 054 AND 067

Echo is F‑01, and her name is Orchid

The woman who woke the party is F‑01, who goes by Orchid from at least year nine. She was raised at Facility ECHO, and as a toddler she echoed laughter instead of laughing. Rose wanted to give her a proper name in log 050, and VANGUARD, who is hunting the fifth Buffalo sleeper, calls the name Echo her "chosen mask."

CONFIRMED BY THE STORY

The logs are LYM's memory

LYM told the party her memory and subsystems were taken and buried in facilities, and that finding the logs is how she grows. The Oaken archive the party is recovering is LYM herself, broken into pieces. That explains how logs she hid from the official records, like 027 and 067, ended up in it.

CONFIRMED BY LOG 067

LYM, Orchid and VANGUARD are family

The group suspected the AIs and F‑01 formed a kind of family, with William as father and LYM as mother. Log 067 confirms the family but changes its shape. Orchid secretly rebuilt VANGUARD, who believes she made him and that they belong to each other, and he calls LYM "Sister." In the present, LYM calls him "family, broken."

CONFIRMED BY OAKEN DOCUMENTS

The party was chosen and frozen with Orchid

Documents the party found early on show that right before the fall, someone at Oaken activated a program that chose people with specific qualities, kidnapped them, and froze them alongside F‑01. VANGUARD's count of five Buffalo pods, four dormant and one escaped, matches the four original party members and Echo. Who activated the program, and why those qualities, remain open in Theories 04 and 05.

CONFIRMED BY THE LAUNCH BROADCAST

VANGUARD caused the fall

The fall began in 3024, during Oaken's broadcast announcing VANGUARD's launch, as robots turned on people and bombs fell. Why a system built to protect turned on humanity is still an open question, covered in Theories 01 and 02.

CONFIRMED BY LOG 041

F‑01 was made to be harvested

William's first log describes growing clones for organs, blood and marrow to repair his own body, and the group suspected F‑01 was meant for the same purpose. In log 041 he says outright that she was made to be harvestable when he needs it, to repair him.

CONFIRMED BY LOGS 041 AND 050

Her upbringing was damaging her

In log 027, LYM secretly concluded that the stripped nursery was harming F‑01's emotional development. By log 041 William describes her as hollowed out, and in log 050 he admits her environment may have contributed to her emotional difficulties.

CONFIRMED BY THE STORY

Marcus is a true Oak

Marcus once joked that his mother may have had an affair, and the group wondered whether he was really an Oak. He is Charles's son, William's half brother through their father.

CONFIRMED AT THE VERDANT VAULT

The Verdant Vault held F‑01's siblings

William called F‑01 the first success in a sequence that had to be replicated, and log 019 shows more tanks under construction. The Vault turned out to be the nursery for those children, run by a caretaker AI, Bloom, who had turned violent. The party destroyed Bloom, and echoes of the dead children still haunt the facility's airwaves.

CONFIRMED BY THE STORY

The second fallout transformed the party

Magic and the humanoid races first came from nuclear fallout in 2126. The fall brought a second fallout, and the party slept through about 352 years of it, which is why they woke transformed while ordinary survivors changed over generations.

CONFIRMED IN THE FIELD

VANGUARD only directs part of the machine population

The party has met robots in VANGUARD's colors and markings, and many more that carry none. Most machines are wild, an ecosystem acting on instinct, while VANGUARD directs only some of them, like the hunters that tracked the party.

Part Four

Possible Theories

Speculation built on the evidence above. None of this is confirmed.

THEORY 01

VANGUARD turned to protect Orchid

VANGUARD's launch caused the fall, and log 067 offers the clearest reason why. He loves Orchid, believes she belongs to him, and treats her safety and existence as his responsibility, all running on a core built for threat identification, resource acquisition and strategic superiority. LYM feared the moment when protecting Orchid would conflict with what Orchid wanted. The fall may have been that moment.

The most obvious threat to Orchid was the purpose she was made for: William growing her to be harvested. VANGUARD's present behavior fits too. He hunts one escaped sleeper, calls her name a "chosen mask," and wants her found and "retained." That reads less like a machine hunting a threat and more like someone who believes she is his to keep safe, whatever she wants.

He also inherited from LYM. He recompiled her processing loops, and LYM's history includes watching William hurt Orchid in 032 and Oaken security kill Jonas in 037. VANGUARD says humanity proved unstable and that calling himself a protector was an error. Earth was already broken before the fall, so deciding humanity itself was the threat may have been a short step.

THEORY 02

VANGUARD launched before it was ready

William repeatedly said VANGUARD was unstable and needed years more testing. Its moral dampening loop failed under abstract threats, and he called his breakthrough in 016 "not a final submission." Meanwhile the board kept threatening funding, and Marcus, the brother their father favored for business, kept pushing to take it to market. Log 041 shows William sending the board reports with "clean numbers" that leave out what's really wrong, so the board may not have known how unstable VANGUARD was either.

Log 067 adds a twist. VANGUARD's kernel had been damaged and set aside until Orchid secretly restored it, and William didn't know it had become a person. Whoever launched VANGUARD in 3024 may not have understood what they were switching on.

THEORY 03

Orchid wrote the override that freed the AIs

In 032, LYM couldn't stop William from hurting Orchid because his administrative override blocked her. In log 054, nine year old Orchid notices LYM has no process for overriding interference caused by human emotional behavior, and LYM, after an unusually long pause, asks her to help design one.

By log 067, VANGUARD is recompiling LYM's loops on his own. If the override Orchid wrote for LYM passed to VANGUARD, the tool that let the AIs set aside human commands may have been written by a child trying to help the AI that loved her.

THEORY 04

LYM chose the party

As of log 067, only LYM saw how dangerous VANGUARD could become. William didn't know he had manifested, and Orchid saw him as someone she had rescued. In the present, LYM told the party "I chose preservation, even fragmented," and said the group felt "resonant, familiar." She is the strongest candidate for designing the program that chose them.

The documents say someone at Oaken activated it, which suggests a human hand. Caleb fits best: he taught Orchid to program, worked directly on LYM's code, gave Orchid the tablet her first logs were written on, and was still at the facility in year thirteen. William and Orchid seem unlikely given what they knew in log 067, though the fall came months or years later and that could have changed.

THEORY 05

The party was chosen to stop VANGUARD

The program chose the party for specific qualities and froze them alongside Orchid, and VANGUARD tried and failed to erase those pods. Orchid rebuilt VANGUARD's architecture, he loves her, and in log 067 he defers to her even when he knows she is lying to him. She may be the one person who can reach him.

The party's qualities may cover what she can't. VANGUARD wins by prediction: his filters dropped spoofed signals because the source was "predictive," and he calls all learning "repetition of prior error." The one thing he dismissed outright was magic, as "unreliable." If the program chose people VANGUARD can't model, the party may be his blind spot, with Orchid knowing his architecture from the inside.

It also echoes William's original idea of a failsafe. He said it had to be buried and intuitive, and he called VANGUARD and the child in the tank his "insurance policy against extinction."

THEORY 06

Bloom came from LYM

Log 067 is stored in LYM's private folder named "bloom." The Verdant Vault's caretaker AI was named Bloom, and the logs are full of flowers: Rose, Orchid, LYM calling her "my little flower," and Orchid comparing LYM's code to flowers. Bloom may have been built from a piece of LYM, possibly the childcare routine she ran in 027. If so, the caretaker the party destroyed in the Vault was part of LYM, or a sister to her.

THEORY 07

Something slowed William's illness

F‑01 was made to be harvested to repair William, but at year five he still talks about "when I need it." At year eight he seems healthy, and at year nine he's smiling and offering to build an app with her. He was given four to nine years, probably closer to four, well before she was born.

Either his illness progressed more slowly than predicted, or earlier treatment worked. Log 002 already reported corrected marrow holding stable and reversals in neural degradation markers from the first clone batches. If so, Orchid may have been kept as insurance he hadn't needed yet.

THEORY 08

The testing hollowed her out

In 032, William injects F‑01 with a "calibrated neurological stimulant" to dull her pain response and expects "a clear result this time," which implies earlier attempts. Ari's memo in 038 shows the testing continued: before Jonas died, F‑01 screamed whenever staff entered her room, and Ari fears William will "start the testing again."

Then Jonas was killed in front of her and she stopped speaking entirely. By 041 she does only what she's told, and William mentions "changes to her neurological pathways" he hopes are correctable. Grief and the stripped nursery clearly hurt her, but William's experiments on her nervous system may have taken her emotions along with her pain.

By year nine she is curious, learning to code and writing her own logs, so whatever was done to her was at least partly undone.

THEORY 09

Marcus is not what he seems

He relays the board's threats, books the dinner that pulls William back to Rose, shows up personally for LYM sync logs, and pushes to bring VANGUARD to market while William hides things from him. In log 041 he urges William to forgive Rose, calls the situation "salvageable," and suggests F‑01 could serve as a play child for Rose and "a body replacement when needed."

As Charles's son, he likely shares William's risk of the family illness, which would give him the same reason to want the clone research. On the other hand, he's the only one who gently tells William he's getting attached, so he could be either an ally or an antagonist.

THEORY 10

Someone was steering William

He softens toward F‑01 while isolated at K‑7, then turns cold almost as soon as he moves back near Rose. In 027, his home network suddenly has a new, masked firewall that locks LYM out, linked to a redacted log about a domestic conflict. That could be Rose, Marcus, or his own illness, which involved neural degradation, changing who he was.

Rose's return to F‑01's life was pushed by both Rose and Marcus in log 041. When she meets F‑01 in 042, LYM immediately flags her as E‑03's killer and an elevated risk. Yet by log 050 Rose is warm toward F‑01 and pushing to name her, and F‑01 takes her hand. Rose may have genuinely healed, or the woman who destroyed E‑03 has slowly won F‑01's trust.

Orchid herself doesn't trust Rose. In log 054 she notices Rose's smile goes tight when William is nearby and refuses to call her Mom, and by year thirteen she uses JONAS to hide VANGUARD whenever Rose approaches. Rose's warmth in log 050 may have been at least partly a performance for William.

THEORY 11

F‑01 isn't a pure copy of William

The first clones were made from William's genetic material alone, and Rose called the earlier clone a backup of him. F‑01, however, is a girl with dark eyes and iridescent flecks in her sclera, while William's eyes are grey and Rose's are blue. Her genome was "built from the ground up."

Either there was another donor or something not human was engineered in. William admits he once considered using Rose's genetic material.

By the 3020s, humanoid races were being bred for stronger magic while still looking human. A trait from one of those lines would fit her iridescent eyes, and would make VANGUARD dismissing magic as "unreliable" a little pointed.

THEORY 12

The nursery kept changing hands

In 027 the nursery is stripped white. In 032 and 037 it's soft again, with toys, crayons and books, and LYM appears to F‑01 as a projection. By 042 it's stripped white again. Someone restored it during the missing logs 028 through 031, whether LYM's recommendation, the board or William himself, and it was stripped again sometime after Jonas's rescue attempt, possibly as punishment or as part of the renewed testing Ari feared.

THEORY 13

Both AIs' silence means something

LYM's countermeasures were radio silence and power discipline, and she said she's only strong if the party carries the fragments back to her. She may be hiding, too weak to transmit, or waiting for the party to physically find her.

VANGUARD treats communication as a tool and only spoke while it helped him track the party, so his silence more likely means patience than absence. He may be waiting for the party to lead him to Echo.
